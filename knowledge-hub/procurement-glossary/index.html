Procurement glossary: procurement terms, explained

70 procurement terms used in Indian industrial, MRO and indirect buying, from ARC and NFA to GRN, e-way bill and three-way match, explained in plain language.

ABCDEFGHIJKLMNOPQRSTUVWXYZ

Tax and legal definitions here are summaries for general understanding. Thresholds and rules change, so confirm current requirements with your finance, tax or legal advisers.

A

Anti-sniping

A reverse-auction rule that extends the closing time when a bid arrives in the final minutes, so other bidders have a fair chance to respond.

Approval matrix

A table that sets who must approve a purchase, based on factors such as value, category and whether the item is on contract. It puts the Delegation of Authority into practice.

Designing an approval matrix

ARC

Annual rate contract: prices agreed with a vendor for a period, usually a year.

Read the ARC procurement guide

Audit trail

A time-stamped record of who did what, and when, on a requisition, approval, order or contract.

B

Blanket purchase order

A purchase order that covers repeated deliveries of agreed items over a period, often up to a value limit, with call-offs placed against it.

C

Call-off

An individual order placed against a rate contract or blanket purchase order.

Catalogue buying

Ordering from a pre-approved list of items with agreed prices and vendors, instead of sourcing each requirement separately.

Category management

Managing spend by groups of related items, such as electricals or PPE, with a plan for each category's vendors, contracts and demand.

Comparative statement (CS)

A table that sets vendor quotes side by side, line by line and on a like-for-like basis, used to identify L1 and to support the NFA.

Contract compliance

The extent to which purchases follow agreed contracts: the contracted vendor, the contracted item and the contracted rate.

Cost centre

A unit of the organisation, such as a department or site, to which spend is charged for budgeting and reporting.

D

Debit note

A document raised by a buyer to record an amount to be recovered from, or adjusted with, a vendor, for example for rejected goods or a short supply.

Delivery challan

A document that accompanies goods in transit when no tax invoice is issued at the time of movement, for example goods sent for job work.

Direct procurement

Buying the materials that go into the product a company makes, such as raw materials and components.

DoA

Delegation of Authority: who in the organisation can approve what, and up to which value.

Designing an approval matrix

E

E-invoice

Under GST, a B2B invoice reported to the Invoice Registration Portal, which validates it and returns an Invoice Reference Number (IRN) and a QR code. It applies to businesses above a notified turnover threshold.

E-procurement

Running purchasing digitally, from requisition and approval through sourcing to purchase order, instead of on paper, email and spreadsheets.

E-procurement vs traditional procurement

E-way bill

An electronic document generated on the GST e-way bill portal before goods above a prescribed value are moved, recording the consignment, consignor, consignee and transport details.

Earnest money deposit (EMD)

A refundable deposit that some tenders require bidders to submit with their bid, as security that the bidder will honour it.

Ex-works (EXW)

A price basis where the vendor makes the goods available at its own premises, and the buyer arranges and pays for transport and bears the risk from there.

F

FOR destination

A price basis common in Indian contracts, where the vendor's rate includes delivery to the buyer's named site.

G

GRN

Goods receipt note: the record made when goods are received and checked at stores, confirming what arrived against the PO.

GST

Goods and Services Tax: India's tax on the supply of goods and services, levied as CGST with SGST (or UTGST) on supplies within a state or union territory, and as IGST on supplies between states.

GSTIN

Goods and Services Tax Identification Number: the 15-character GST registration number of a business.

H

HSN code

Harmonised System of Nomenclature code: the classification code for goods used on GST invoices and to determine the applicable tax rate.

I

Incoterms

Standard trade terms published by the International Chamber of Commerce that set out who pays for, and bears the risk of, each stage of delivery. Mostly used in import and export contracts.

Indent

A common Indian term for an internal request for material, raised by a user department or stores. Similar to a purchase requisition.

Indicative quantity

An estimate of the quantity to be bought over a contract period, shared with vendors for pricing, without a firm commitment to buy it.

Indirect procurement

Buying the goods and services that keep a business running but don't go into its product, such as MRO, office supplies, IT and facility services.

Input tax credit (ITC)

The GST paid on purchases that a registered business can set off against the GST it owes on its own supplies, subject to the conditions in GST law.

Item master

The list of item codes and descriptions in an ERP or procurement system. Duplicate or inconsistent entries make spend hard to analyse and contracts hard to enforce.

K

KAM

Key Account Manager: the named person at a supplier or platform who manages a client's account. At ProcEaze, your KAM loads your ARC prices and tracks renewals.

L

L1 / L2 / L3

The lowest, second-lowest and third-lowest bids or quotes for a requirement.

Landed cost

The total cost of getting an item to your site: the price plus freight, packing, insurance, duties and other charges.

Lead time

The time between placing an order and receiving the goods.

Letter of intent (LOI)

A document telling a vendor that you intend to award them an order or contract, issued before the formal PO or contract.

M

Maverick spend

Purchases made outside agreed contracts, vendors or processes. Also called off-contract spend.

Why negotiated rates leak

MOQ

Minimum order quantity for an item.

MRO

Maintenance, repair and operations: the supplies that keep plants and facilities running.

MRO and the long tail

MSME

Micro, small and medium enterprises, classified by investment and turnover and registered on the Udyam portal. Payments to micro and small suppliers are subject to time limits under the MSMED Act, 2006.

N

NFA

Note for Approval: the document that seeks approval for a sourcing decision before a PO is issued.

What goes into an NFA

P

PAN

Permanent Account Number: the 10-character income-tax identifier, collected from vendors during onboarding.

Payment terms

When and how a vendor will be paid, for example a set number of days from receipt of goods or invoice.

PO

Purchase order: the formal order issued to a vendor.

PR

Purchase requisition: an internal request to buy, raised before a PO.

Price basis

What a quoted rate includes, such as freight, packing, loading and insurance, and whether taxes are extra.

Price-variation clause (PVC)

A contract clause that adjusts a rate in line with an agreed index or base price, using a set formula and review frequency. Common for items linked to raw materials such as copper or steel.

ARC commercial terms

Procure-to-pay (P2P)

The process from purchase requisition through approval, purchase order, goods receipt and invoice to payment.

Procure-to-pay vs source-to-pay

R

Rate contract

An agreement that fixes rates for listed items for a period, without committing to a fixed quantity. When it runs for a year, it is called an annual rate contract (ARC).

Rate contract vs spot buying

Request for information (RFI)

An early request asking vendors to describe their capabilities, used to build a shortlist before an RFQ or RFP.

Request for proposal (RFP)

An invitation to vendors to propose both an approach and a price, used where the solution matters as much as the price.

Reverse auction

An auction where vendors bid prices down, and the lowest bid ranks first.

Reverse auctions on ProcEaze

RFQ

Request for quotation: an invitation to vendors to quote for a requirement.

S

Segregation of duties

Splitting key steps, such as requesting, approving, ordering and receiving, between different people, so no one person controls a purchase from end to end.

Single source

Buying from one chosen vendor even though others could supply. Different from sole source, where only one vendor can supply.

Sole source

A purchase where only one vendor can supply the item, for example a proprietary spare available only from the original manufacturer.

Source-to-pay (S2P)

The full process from spend analysis and sourcing through contracting and supplier management to procure-to-pay.

Procure-to-pay vs source-to-pay

Spend analysis

Collecting and classifying purchase data by category, vendor, site and item, to see where money goes and where to act.

Spend leakage

Value lost when purchases don't follow negotiated contracts: the wrong vendor, a higher price than agreed, or an item bought outside the contract.

Why negotiated rates leak

Spend under management

The share of spend covered by procurement processes such as contracts, approved vendors and competitive sourcing.

Spot buying

Buying each requirement as it arises, usually on fresh quotes, rather than under a standing contract.

Rate contract vs spot buying

Supplier evaluation

Assessing vendors against set criteria, such as quality, delivery, price, compliance and financial standing, before or during a contract.

T

Tail spend

The many small, infrequent purchases spread across many vendors. Individually low in value, but often where process cost is highest relative to what is bought.

MRO and the long tail

TDS on purchase of goods

Tax deducted at source by a buyer on payments to a resident seller for goods, which applies when the buyer's turnover and the year's purchases from that seller cross thresholds set in income-tax law. Confirm the current rate, thresholds and section with your tax adviser.

Techno-commercial evaluation

Evaluating bids on technical compliance first and commercial terms second, often in a two-bid process where price bids are opened only for technically qualified vendors.

Three-way match

Checking that the PO, the goods receipt (GRN) and the vendor invoice agree on item, quantity and price before payment is released.

Total cost of ownership (TCO)

The full cost of an item over its life, including price, delivery, installation, maintenance, downtime and disposal.

V

Vendor master

The record of approved vendors in an ERP or procurement system, with details such as GSTIN, PAN, bank account and payment terms.

Vendor onboarding

Registering and verifying a new vendor, including documents such as GSTIN, PAN and bank details, before they can receive orders.

W

Work order

An order for services or works, used in place of a purchase order for jobs such as maintenance, installation or civil works.
