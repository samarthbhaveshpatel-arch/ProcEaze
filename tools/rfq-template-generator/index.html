What is an RFQ template?

A request for quotation asks suppliers to price a defined requirement on defined terms. A template makes sure every RFQ your team sends carries the same essentials: a reference number, line items with specifications and units, the delivery location, a due date, and the commercial terms you expect. Suppliers quote on the same basis, so you can compare like with like.

This generator also adds standard instructions to suppliers: quote in rupees per unit with GST shown separately, state the make offered and any deviation from specification, give the lead time and quote validity, and include GSTIN and PAN.

How to use the generator

  1. Enter your company, an RFQ number your team can trace, and a short title.
  2. Add a line for each item. Be specific in the specification: standard, grade, size, approved makes.
  3. Set the delivery location, quote due date and quote validity.
  4. Choose or type your payment and delivery terms, and say how quotes will be evaluated.
  5. Check the preview, then print it, copy it into an email, or download it as .txt or .html.

The HTML download opens in any browser and in Word if you want to add your letterhead.

Tips

Writing an RFQ suppliers can price accurately.

  • One requirement per line. Combining items in one line makes quotes impossible to compare.
  • State the unit of measure and pack size explicitly: “Box of 100” is not the same as “Nos”.
  • Tell suppliers how you will evaluate quotes. It reduces follow-up questions and makes the award easier to justify.
  • Ask for GST, freight and packing separately, so you compare total landed cost.
  • Give a realistic due date and stick to it. Late quotes accepted for one supplier are unfair to the rest.
  • Keep the RFQ number on every quote, comparison and approval so the trail is easy to audit.
