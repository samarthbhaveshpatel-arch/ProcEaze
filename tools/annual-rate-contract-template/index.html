Structure

What goes into an annual rate contract.

Eleven parts every ARC should settle. The downloadable template follows the same order, plus definitions and general terms for your legal team.

  1. 01

    Parties

    Legal names, registered addresses, GSTIN and PAN of the buyer and supplier, and the authorised contacts on each side.

  2. 02

    Period and validity

    Start and end dates, whether POs issued inside the period are honoured after it ends, and that quantities are estimates.

  3. 03

    Item schedule

    Every item with its specification, make, unit of measure, HSN code, GST rate, MOQ, estimated annual quantity and rate.

  4. 04

    Price basis and taxes

    Whether rates include GST, freight, packing and insurance, and the delivery basis (for example FOR destination or ex-works).

  5. 05

    Price variation

    Fixed for the period, linked to a named index with a cap, or revised only on documented request and written approval.

  6. 06

    Delivery

    Lead time from PO date, delivery locations, documents with each delivery, and when title and risk pass.

  7. 07

    Payment

    Credit period from receipt of accepted material and a correct invoice, statutory deductions and bank details.

  8. 08

    Quality and rejection

    Conformance to specification, inspection rights, replacement of rejected items and warranty or shelf life.

  9. 09

    Penalties

    Liquidated damages for late delivery, with a cap, and the right to buy elsewhere at the supplier's risk and cost.

  10. 10

    Termination

    Notice period, termination for breach or repeated failures, and what happens to open purchase orders.

  11. 11

    Renewal

    How and when the contract is extended or renewed, and whether rates carry over.

What an annual rate contract does

An annual rate contract fixes the price and terms for items you buy repeatedly: safety equipment, fasteners, bearings, electrical consumables, stationery. Instead of collecting quotations for every order, you run one competitive process, agree rates for the year, and then order against the contract with purchase orders as needs arise.

The value of an ARC depends on two things: how carefully the item schedule is written, and whether buyers actually order at the contracted rate. A vague specification lets a different make arrive at the same price; a missing price basis turns freight into an argument at invoice time.

How to use this template

  1. List the repeat items from your consumption history in the CSV and write a clear specification for each.
  2. Send the schedule with an RFQ to qualified suppliers and compare quotes on the same basis.
  3. Fill in the agreed rates, MOQ, lead times and validity.
  4. Use the contract structure to agree the commercial terms, then pass it to your legal team for drafting.
  5. Publish the contracted items where buyers order, and set a reminder for renewal.

For a fuller walk-through, read the guide to annual rate contract procurement.

Tips

Getting more from a rate contract.

  • Write specifications you can inspect against: standard, grade, size and approved makes, not just a product name.
  • Keep GST, freight and packing in separate columns so quotes compare on the same basis.
  • State that quantities are estimates unless you genuinely intend to commit to a minimum offtake.
  • Agree a price-variation rule up front. Silence invites mid-year revision requests.
  • Track how much spend actually goes through the contract. An ARC nobody orders against saves nothing.
  • Start renewal early enough to retender and approve before the old rates expire.
