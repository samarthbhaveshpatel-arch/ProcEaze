Why MRO buying needs its own checklist

MRO spend behaves differently from direct materials. Instead of a few large orders, it is thousands of small ones: a pack of gloves here, a bearing there, a cable drum for a shutdown. Requests come from maintenance, stores, projects, administration and every site. That spread is why small process gaps matter. A duplicate item code, a missing MOQ or one unapproved supplier repeats across hundreds of orders.

The checklist follows the order the work happens in, so you can see which stage is weakest and fix that first.

How to use the checklist

  1. Pick a scope: one category, one site or all MRO.
  2. Go stage by stage and tick only what is genuinely in place today.
  3. Watch the stage counts: the stage with the fewest ticks is usually where to start.
  4. Print the checklist for a review meeting, and repeat it each quarter.

If you are not sure what a term means, see the procurement glossary. For rate contracts, read the guide to annual rate contract procurement.

Tips

Quick wins in MRO procurement.

  • Start with item master hygiene. Clean codes and specifications make every later stage easier.
  • Put the top repeat items on rate contracts first; the long tail can follow.
  • Make the contracted catalogue the easiest way to buy, so buyers choose it without being told.
  • Review off-contract purchases monthly with the departments that raise them.
  • Keep requisition, approval, PO, receipt and invoice linked, so audits take hours rather than weeks.
