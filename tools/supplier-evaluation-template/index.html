    Default criteria

    Seven criteria to start from.

    Sensible starting weights for indirect and MRO items. They are a starting point, not a standard: change them to suit the category before you score.

    Default supplier evaluation criteria and weights
    CriterionDefault weightWhat to assess
    Price / total cost30%Landed cost: price plus freight, packing and taxes, and the effect of payment terms.
    Quality20%Conformance to specification, rejection history, test certificates.
    Delivery reliability15%On-time delivery record, lead time, ability to handle urgent orders.
    Technical compliance15%Meets every mandatory specification; deviations are listed and acceptable.
    Payment terms5%Credit period and conditions offered.

    Why weighted supplier evaluation?

    Choosing on price alone is quick, but it hides the cost of late deliveries, rejected batches and missing documents. A weighted evaluation makes the trade-offs explicit: everyone can see that price carried 30% of the decision and quality 20%, and how each supplier scored. That makes the award easier to explain to approvers and auditors, and fairer to suppliers.

    It works best when the criteria and weights are fixed before quotes are opened, and when more than one person scores.

    How to use the template

    1. Review the criteria. Rename, add or remove them to fit the category.
    2. Set the weights so they total exactly 100%. The total is shown live.
    3. Name your suppliers (two to five) and score each criterion from 1 to 5.
    4. Read the weighted scores and ranking. Check any supplier with unscored criteria.
    5. Download the CSV and attach it to your comparison or note for approval.

    Tips

    Scoring suppliers consistently.

    • Treat mandatory requirements, such as a required certification, as pass or fail before you score.
    • Write a one-line definition of 1, 3 and 5 for each criterion so evaluators score the same way.
    • Score price on total landed cost, not the unit rate alone.
    • Use delivery and quality records from past orders where you have them, rather than impressions.
    • If two suppliers are within a few tenths of a point, look again at the highest-weighted criteria before deciding.
